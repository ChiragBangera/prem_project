# Features

What the app does, page by page. The [README](../README.md) has the short version and how to run it.

## The pages

Every page opens with findings, then the evidence.

- **Briefing**: what stands out right now, beside the table at a glance (points, how far they sit from what the chances earned, form): teams above or below what their chances deserve, players riding luck, the title race, the latest results with who scored and how the chances split, the next fixtures with each side's position, form and chance difference, the biggest movers, and your shortlist. Each finding draws its numbers on one scale (what happened against what it is measured against, or a score out of 100), and a caveat every finding shares, such as a small sample early in a season, is said once under the set.
- **League**: the standings on the same table as Scout, with three tabs (**Table**, **Expected**, **Style**). Headings use the team catalog's names (P, W, GF, xPts, PPDA ...): point at one for its full name, click it for its spread across the league and to sort. Cells are shaded by rank in the league; the luck columns (points minus expected points, goals minus xG, xGA minus goals against) are drawn as bars either side of zero, scaled to the largest gap in the table; Champions League and relegation places are a stripe down the first column. Home or away and the last 5 or 10 games re-make the table. Then attack against defence, and the table race matchweek by matchweek.
- **Matches**: every match of a matchweek as a compact card: big score, scorers and minutes, an xG bar that shows at a glance who made the better chances, possession and shots, and a flag on the ones that went against them. **Pick a team** for its whole season instead: its next three games, every result (latest first, with its record, goals and xG for and against above), then the rest of the fixtures. **Results that lied** works for every team or one. Open a card for the match page (below).
- **Match page**: a scoreboard (score, half-time score, xG, possession, shots, ground, crowd and referee) over six sections, each with its own address:
  - **Overview**: what stands out in words; **Momentum** (who was on top minute by minute, with the goals, cards and substitutions on the same clock); the xG race beside who made the chances; **the match in numbers** for the whole match or either half (attack, on the ball, without it: field tilt, entries into the box, progressive passes, regains high up, passes allowed per pressing action ...); and who led the match at what.
  - **Shots**: the **shot board** (every shot on the pitch and in a list beside it; pick a player to see only his shots, pick a shot for its xG, taker, body part, situation and assist; a shot with no xG is drawn with a dashed outline and never estimated), where the shots went in each goal as the shooter saw it, and when the chances came.
  - **Passing**: both sides on two pitches drawn the same way round: the pass network of the starting eleven, touches, progressive passes, passes into the final third and into the box, chances created, crosses and carries; which side each team attacked down; and possession and field tilt quarter-hour by quarter-hour.
  - **Moves**: the match cut into spells of possession. Every move in order, filtered by side, by how it ended (a goal, any shot, lost, out of play), by how many passes it had and whether it reached the box. The move you pick is drawn on a pitch with each action numbered in order and named, carries dotted, and the place it was taken marked with who took it; under it the same move is written out line by line ("1. Rice → Saka"). Then each side's moves in numbers, who lost the ball most and who broke up most moves, and a map of where each side's moves broke down. The arrow keys step from move to move.
  - **Defending**: where each side won the ball back, as marks or as a heat map, with the average height of its engagement, its regains high up and its PPDA.
  - **Line-ups**: both elevens on one pitch in their shapes, with scorers, bookings and substitutions marked, and what came from the bench.
  - **Players**: a quick table per team, **Deep analytics** (below), and every player's event numbers for either half.
  Momentum, Passing, Moves, Defending and Line-ups come from the event data, so they need it stored for the match; the page says so where it is not. Nothing in them is a model: they are counts, and the two estimates (who received a pass, and carries) say that they are. A match's address carries its league and season, so a link opens the right match whatever league the app was left on.
- **Live matches**: a match being played (or just finished, before Understat lists it) says so on its card (Live, Half time, Full time). Once WhoScored has been read (at half time for the matches you follow, from the 90th minute for every match), opening it shows **the same match page**, drawn from that read: the scoreboard with the minute, momentum, the numbers of the half, shots and both goal mouths, passing, defending, line-ups and every player's line. Only the parts that need xG wait: they fill in when Understat publishes after full time; nothing is estimated. The page refreshes itself every minute until Understat has the match, and then is Understat's own report. Before the first read (and for any match not played yet) the page shows where the match is by the clock, when it will be read, and **the season so far, side by side**: each side's place, record, form and run of xG difference, and the two compared on points, xG for and against, goals, deep completions and pressing. A description, not a prediction. Opening a match while it is being played follows it: it is read at half time as well as full time.
- **Scout**: find players by what they do (below).
- **Teams**: the same desk for teams: every team measure, ranked within its own league and season. The rail filters by usual formation and by a rule on any measure, with the team ready-made searches (High press, Possession-dominant, Set-piece reliant ...); a row opens the team beside the list (place, points against expected points, form, manager, and its rank in the league on results, chances and style, style in grey).
- **Deep analytics** (Match page, Players): every player who played, from both teams, on the same desk as Scout, with every Scout measure worked out for that match alone. The rail filters by side, role and position, minutes in the match, who started or came on, and a rule on any measure; it opens on players with 30+ minutes (shown as a filter pill, one click to remove). Cells are shaded by where the number would rank among this season's players of the same role; players under 30 minutes are not ranked. The best and the weakest number of each measure among the players listed is ringed, so who led at what reads at a glance. A row opens the player beside the list; Table / Map as in Scout. Its state lives in the page, not the address (the match's tabs use that). The quick side-by-side view stays as it was.
- **Team page**: **Overview** (every match as chances against results, each bar named with the opponent and the score, what is next, how they play), **Players** (the squad on the same desk as Scout, inside the page: filters, ready-made searches, any columns, one player beside the list, the map, and where the output comes from), **Style & maps** (a style profile and pitch maps), **Chances** (where chances come from and where they are allowed, one chart with toggles), **Matches** (match by match, and whether having the ball helps) and **History** (seasons side by side). **Add to favourites** in its header follows the team: its matches are read at half time as well as full time and carry a star on the match lists.
- **Player page**: a percentile **profile at a glance** (sixteen measures in four groups, one slice each: the shape of a player before any number), then Profile against role peers (every metric is searchable and every column of the table sorts, flat or inside each kind; the bars say who he is compared with and what a full bar, an empty bar and the tick mean), a **Match trend**, pitch **Maps**, a **Match log**, **Finishing and shots** (an exact "how unusual is his finishing" distribution and the same shot board as a match, each shot linking to the match it came in), **Seasons** and statistically **Similar players**.
- **Match trend** (Player page): any metric he has, match by match, for this season or an earlier one. One mark per match, labelled with the opponent and shaded by how strong that opponent was (its place in the table by expected points, so a strong run against weak sides can be told from real form), with the season-to-date line (its last point is the number on his profile), his last five appearances, and the middle half of his role's season figures behind them. Hover a match for the opponent, result, minutes and how much it moved his season figure; click it for the match report. Below: what stands out in words, the metric pooled against the top, middle and bottom third of the league and at home and away, and every match in a table that sorts by how much it moved the season. A match with no stored page, or one he did not play, is a gap, never a zero. A metric in the profile table has a small trend icon that opens it here.
- **Compare**: players (their percentile shapes on one radar, then a percentile dot plot with the differences spelled out) or two teams (numbers, style with the measures grouped and named in full, trend, meetings).
- **Shortlist**: players you track on the same table as Scout (column sets, heading menus, shading by rank in the role), your own note on each as a column that saves itself, a row that opens the player beside the list, and a tray to compare them. A player with no minutes in the season shown stays on the list with his note. Favourite teams are kept the same way, in the app's database: restarts, updates and clearing the cache keep both.
- **Dictionary**: every raw field and every derived number: what it is, its formula, where it comes from, what it needs, how to read it, and what a typical value looks like. Plus every event type and qualifier, every profile tag and lens in words.
- **Guide** and **Data**: where to find things, and what is stored, what the updater did, what failed, and how complete each layer is.

**Light and dark.** The button in the top bar (Auto, Light, Dark) switches the theme; Auto follows the computer's setting. In the light theme cards are white on a cooler grey ground with one clear border, the pitch is a deeper green, and a warning (saved data, a failed refresh) is a quiet strip with an amber edge instead of a filled amber band. Text colours meet WCAG AA (4.5:1) on every surface.

**Rows end on one line.** Cards that share a row are the same height (the shorter one centres what it holds). On the Team overview the manager table appears only when the season had more than one manager (one manager is already in the heading), and the five-match average of chances takes over from the match-by-match line once eight matches are played. On the Player profile, how he was classified (**Role**) closes the page as reference. A player's findings open his Profile tab (not every tab), his finishing verdict and shot facts share one card, and on a phone the Briefing shows three findings before the table, which fits the screen without Form.

**When a source is down.** The Data page opens on three answers: whether the data is up to date (and since when), what the updater is doing, and what needs you. If Understat is down the page says so in its own words, the app waits (5 minutes, then 10, 20 … up to 6 hours) instead of asking every minute, pages keep showing what is stored without asking again, and the status pill in the top bar turns amber once live tables are more than two days old. If the event fetcher stops, the page shows the fetcher's own last words (for example which piece is missing and the command that installs it), not just an exit code.

**Update now** on the Data page says what the press will do (which tables it asks for, how many match pages and matches of event data, and about how long that takes) and tries again at once anything that was waiting after a failure. **Event data on an Apple-silicon Mac without Rosetta** works by itself: the fetcher prepares a driver that Mac can run. Event data is also fetched for matches the league table does not list yet (when Understat is slow or down), going by the fixture list's kickoff times.

Press `/` or `Ctrl`/`⌘` + `K` anywhere to search players, teams and pages. The address bar carries the page's state (filters, sort, columns, scope), so any view can be bookmarked or sent to someone.

## Scout and Teams: nothing is pre-selected

**Scout, Teams, a team's Players tab and a match's Deep analytics are laid out as one desk** (the League table and the Shortlist use its table without the rail):

- **Left, the filter rail**, always in view: search, role and position, age and minutes drawn as a histogram of the players being cut with handles on it, rules on any measure ("What he is good at": a slider for rank within the role, or the number itself, at least or at most), the ready-made searches, profile, club, and searches you have named and kept on this computer.
- **Middle, the table, from the top of the screen.** Sets of measures are tabs. Every measure column is the same width with a short heading; pointing at a heading gives the full name and meaning, and clicking it opens a menu: the measure's spread among the players listed, sort either way, keep only the top 10%, 25%, half or the bottom 25% of the role, set an exact limit, hide the column. Cells are shaded by rank within the player's own role (orange for the bottom 15%), so a strong row reads as a darker row. The list scrolls in its own box and Back from a player's page returns to the same row.
- **Right, one player at a glance.** Clicking a row opens him beside the list (profile tags, minutes, role score, eight ranked bars for his role, links to the full profile, compare and shortlist) without leaving it. On a phone the rail folds behind a Filters button and a row opens the player's page.
- **A ready-made search** (what used to be a lens chip) now also shows the measures it looks at and ranks by its main one when switched on, lists its rules in words, and offers "Edit these rules", which turns them into your own rules with movable limits.

Both pages list **everyone** until you narrow them down, and every control is yours:

- **Filters add up**, each one independent. For players: role, exact position, club, minutes, age, playing time and **Profile** (the tags below). For teams: usual formation. For both, a **rule on a measure**: "keep those whose value, or rank, is above or below a limit" on any of the ~110 player (or ~100 team) measures. Each active filter shows as a pill you can remove.
- **Ready-made searches are quick filters, explained.** "Goal threats", "Ball winners", "Unlucky finishers", "Young and good", "Ever-present" (19 for players), "High press", "Possession-dominant", "Set-piece reliant" (13 for teams) ... One is nothing but a set of filter rules; switched on, it lists exactly which rules it applies, shows the measures it looks at and ranks by its main one.
- **Profile tags** describe how a player plays: Poacher, Complete forward, Chance creator, Playmaker, Ball-playing defender, Aerial dominator, Sweeper-keeper ... 27 in all. Each is a short rule on role percentiles that you can read in the Dictionary, and each tag shown on a player says why he earned it.
- **The table is yours too.** Columns come in groups (shooting, creating, passing, defending ...). Pick a ready-made set (Overview, Attacking, Creating, Passing, Carrying, Defending, Duels, Goalkeeping, Set pieces, Discipline) or choose any of the metrics as a column. Click any heading to sort; blanks always sort last, never as zero.
- **Top N** (10, 20, 50, 100 or your own number) applies to the table **and** the map, so "the ten best ball-winners among defenders under 23" is two clicks.
- **The map** plots any metric against any other, coloured by role (players) or league, sized by minutes, with outliers fenced so one cameo cannot flatten the picture. It **starts from a question** ("Who makes and takes chances?", "Busy, or productive?", "Do his runs lead anywhere?", "Dribbles with a point?", "Safe passer, or a forward one?"), each a pair of metrics explained in a sentence. Where the question is volume against what came of it, the map draws **a trend line for each role** and names the players furthest below and above their own role's line.
- **Volume against end product.** Progressive passes, progressive carries, take-ons won, crosses and final-third entries are each counted a second time: how many were in a move that came to a shot (its own, or one from the corner, free kick or penalty it won). That gives a column set ("What came of it"), two lenses ("Busy, little end product", "Progress that counts"), a card on a player's Profile ("What his actions led to": one split bar per kind of action, solid for the part that came to a shot, with the role percentile of that share, plus how often a move ended with him losing the ball and how many of the opponents' moves he ended), and a card on a match's Players tab under By half ("Busy, and what came of it": the busiest players of both sides). It describes actions, not a player's worth, and the pages say so.
- A heading's menu draws the measure's **spread among the rows listed**, and a note appears when a rate is led by players with very few minutes.
- Percentiles always compare a player **only with his own role** (a team only with its own league and season), shrunk toward the average in proportion to how little football stands behind them.
- Tick up to four players to compare, export what you see as CSV, or copy a link to exactly this view.

## Pitch maps (event data)

With event data stored, every player and team has maps drawn from the real positions of every touch: **Touches** (heat, drawn as banded areas where a side played more than usual), **Passes** (progressive, key passes, into the box, long balls, crosses, through balls), **Pass network** (average positions and the strongest links), **Defending** (tackles, interceptions, recoveries, clearances, aerials), **Carries**, **Take-ons**, **Shots** and **Goalkeeper** actions, filterable by home/away and by match. All maps attack left to right with the left wing at the top. Where no events are stored a map says so rather than drawing nothing.

**Moves over a season.** Under the maps, a team's Style & maps tab has "How their moves end": every spell of possession (the same moves a match's Moves tab lists) by how it ended, by where and how it began with the share that came to a shot, a heat map of where the ball is lost in play, who lost it most and who took it off the opponents most. A player's Maps tab has "Moves he was part of": where he lost the ball and how (tackled, a pass that did not arrive, a loose ball), and where he took it off the other side.

**Matches Understat has not published.** A finished match that is stored from WhoScored but missing from Understat (it publishes hours later, and was down for a month in autumn 2026) is shown from WhoScored: the Matches page gives the real score, scorers, shots and possession with "No xG yet", and the match opens with every event tab. xG is left empty, never estimated, and round and team summaries leave it out of their xG totals rather than adding zeros.
